I launched [Poach](https://poach.vc/), a newsletter to help early stage investors find interesting founders, in November 2024. I've published three issues almost every week for just over three months, and Poach has grown to $250 MRR and close to 350 subscribers, and several major investment firms subscribe to the newsletter. Here's a small retrospective on what went into building an initial subscriber base for this newsletter.

## First Paid Customer

The idea for this came from a discussion with an investor. I put the idea on the back burner for several months before coming back to it. I built scripts to find initial users and manually curated them into a couple initial newsletter editions before reaching out to this investor to check if this was what they were thinking of when we discussed before. They said yes and paid for a subscription immediately, so I decided to invest more in finding if there was a market for this kind of publication.

## Cold Outreach

The first thing I did was to reach out over email to a lot of early stage investors. I used online lists of VCs to drive the outreach and tried to automate as much of it as possible. While the response rate was low, this is where the majority of active subscribers and about a third of paid subscribers came from.

## Community Posting

Posting in VC-related Slack groups, LinkedIn groups, Discords, and forums had mixed results based on the specific location. Some places immediately rejected any posts and others drove little traffic. One group in particular resulted in a decent influx of signups and a small amount of traffic. Overall this seemed promising, but there aren't a huge number of active (and public) groups for VCs. 

## Meco for Publishers

There's a newsletter reader app called [Meco](https://meco.app/) with a publisher program. The premise sounds promising: just set a price you're willing to pay to acquire a subscriber and give them a budget and description of your newsletter, and they do the rest. They recommend your newsletter to their users and only charge you for "engaged subscribers", or people that read at least one of your posts after subscribing. I decided to experiment with Meco with a small budget and a slightly higher than average cost per acquisition. 

After a week, subscribers started to roll in. The first downside was that these subscribers used mecoinbox.com emails, which immediately felt less valuable. It's harder to tell demographic data or which VC firms the subscribers belonged to, so it was harder to evaluate the quality of the free subscribers. Also, when looking at the final numbers after stopping, the results weren't great. Meco had 252 accounts subscribe to Poach, of which 118 counted as "engaged subscribers". After importing those subscribers into Substack, however, only 17 actually showed as reading even one more post, and none of them converted to paid. So while this did pad my subscriber numbers, it decreased my open rate for emails and was a failed investment. 

Maybe less niche publications would fare better, but for Poach, this just wasn't cost effective..

## Facebook Ads

I also ran some ads on Facebook. I used the often-memed tip of Marc Andreessen's head at the bottom of a static ad that just described what the newsletter was about. I was able to gain a decent number of global readers, but had similarly low conversions to regular readers as Meco. Since the target audience is concentrated in high cost of living areas and high or ultra high net worth individuals, targeting the ads further was cost prohibitive. At the end of the day, none of these users converted to paid.

## The Future

### Referrals and Gifts

I have started to see some referrals, where existing readers are driving subscriptions or sending a gift subscription to people they know, but this is still early stages. We'll have to see how these end up converting.

### Social Media

I'm also starting to use social media more to promote this. On Twitter, I'm now mentioning all investors that were followed by top VCs during the recent time period. I'm also going to begin doing some cold outreach on Twitter. This is also early days, so it's yet to be determined if this is effective.

### Goals

While working on this, I've tried to get a sense of the size of the market for the current "product", which is just a curated list of founders that top VCs follow on Twitter. In looking for ways to expand revenue, the most obvious opportunities seem to lie in two different areas. The first is to build a SaaS product around finding founders, filtering them down on fairly custom criteria, and alerting VCs of these interesting founders. The second is to find other unique datasets that might be useful for VCs. For example, founder activity on different platforms, meetups, or partnering with companies that serve early stage founders to build a proprietary dataset.

Neither of these are particularly appealing at the moment, since I've decided to focus more on  [KeywordGems](https://keywordgems.com/), my AI-powered SEO researcher. So my current plan is to maintain the posting schedule and slightly expand outreach until I reach around 50 paid subscribers, when I'll start to look for a buyer. Newsletters are getting pretty great revenue multiples lately, and I'm sure I can find someone who is interested in expanding to a SaaS or building new proprietary datasets for VCs.
